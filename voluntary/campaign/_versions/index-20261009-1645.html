Case study

Voluntary

A four-minute zombie comedy sketch for Halloween, built out the way a studio would release a film. Poster, trailer, four standalone shorts, scripts, storyboards, and a month of posts planned from one day of shooting.

Everything on this page was made in pre-production, before a frame was shot, by one person.

  • The filmThere isn't one. The sketch is the whole movie, and the campaign is built as if it weren't.
  • ShootOne day. A living room, a parking lot, and a street in Los Angeles.
  • OutputOne horizontal sketch, four verticals, a trailer, stills and memes, across a month
  • Built withFountain, code-drawn boards, AI-generated concept stills, a Notion and Claude Code back office

Open it
Don't Touch Me

One short, built to be a meme

Sixty seconds. Zombie point of view chasing a woman down a dark street. She turns, screams DON'T TOUCH ME straight down the lens, and the zombie backs off terrified.

Put a different line over the top and it's a different meme. Three are below. If any one of them catches, the public takes it from there.

Concept still, AI-generated in pre-production to lock the frame before casting.

Distribution

One day becomes a month

Every piece is planned so it goes out on its own schedule, stands on its own, and points back at the sketch. Horizontal for YouTube, vertical for everything else, cut from the same takes.

WhenWhat goes out
Week 1The trailer. Played straight, no reveal.Horizontal, plus a vertical crop
Week 2Don't Touch Me, then the two meme versions a few days apart.Vertical
Week 3Prep and The Loud One. Stills from the room between them.Vertical
HalloweenThe sketch. Extra Teeth the morning after.Horizontal, then vertical

The order is a plan, not a rule. Whatever people stay on moves up.

Coverage

Shot for horizontal and vertical at the same time

Three cameras. Every frame is composed for 16:9 first, then each shot is marked with how the vertical comes out of it: crop it, grab an extra take, or split-screen two cameras for the ensemble beats.

The boards are drawn in code, so reblocking is a number change and a new PDF in seconds.

Hooks

Tested, not guessed

There is never just one trailer. Five versions of the first three seconds go out, the one people stay past three seconds on is the one that gets pushed, and the recut is same-day because the edit pipeline and the posting calendar are one system.

That's the same way Crazy Stupid Money runs every week, and it's why one person can keep five platforms moving without an agency.
